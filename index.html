References:

  1. ↑ Unique Words vs. Total Words: This classic statistic (type-token ratio) shows the ratio of unique words used to the total number of words in Eminem's discography (or a specific album). It always drops for longer texts, so compare only selections of similar size, otherwise use MATTR.
  2. ↑ Moving-Average Type-Token Ratio (MATTR): The unique ratio measured in every window of 100 consecutive words and averaged. It doesn't depend on text length, so a short track, a whole album and the full discography can be compared directly. Higher means richer vocabulary.
  3. ↑ New Word Interval (NWI): The frequency of new words. Similar to the stat achieved by Guinness World Records it is the average number of words between each unique word Eminem uses (total / unique). A lower NWI indicates a denser vocabulary usage.
  4. ↑ Repeat Interval: The average distance, in words, between two uses of the same word. Choruses and hooks pull it down, long verses with rare words push it up.
  5. ↑ Hapax Legomena: Words that occur only once in the selection. A large share of one-time words means the vocabulary is wide rather than built on repetition.
  6. ↑ Evolution over Time: The changes in Eminem's vocabulary over his career: the linear trend of per-track MATTR by release year, in percentage points per decade, with the correlation coefficient r (how well the trend explains the data, from -1 to 1) and MATTR of each year.
  7. ↑ Syllable Count: The distribution of words by syllable count (estimated from vowel groups). Does Eminem favor short, punchy words for emphasis, or use a wider range for flow and complexity.
  8. ↑ Character Count: Similar to syllables, if Eminem leans towards shorter, more concise words or incorporates a variety of word lengths for lyrical richness.
  9. ↑ Part-of-Speech Analysis: The distribution of parts of speech (pronouns, verbs, prepositions etc.), based on word lists. Words outside the lists are counted as "other" (mostly nouns and adjectives). This can show lyrical style, focus on storytelling vs. emotions, or aggressiveness vs. introspection.
  10. ↑ Lexical Density: The share of content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) among all words, as opposed to function words like "the", "and", "you", "is". Dense lyrics carry more information per line.
  11. ↑ Pronoun Focus: First person ("I", "me", "my", "I'm"...) against second person ("you", "your", "ya"...). A high ratio points to introspection and self-narration, a low one to addressing someone: a lover, a hater, the listener.
